## 4. State vocabulary (all directions)

Silhouette first, word always, colour third (DESIGN.md "Evidence states"). Phosphor glyphs, decorative; no new tokens.

| Check | Glyph | Word | Colour | Rule on the item | Body |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Off | not-equals | OFF BY $12,400.00 | `--severity-critical-fg` | 3px solid | printed, rows add up to, off by (per metric) |
| **Couldn't check** (`needs_review`) | dashed circle | COULDN'T CHECK | `--severity-high-fg` | 3px **dashed** | printed figures, "Not checked" where the sum would be, the reason, no difference |
| Conflicting | scales | CONFLICTING | `--color-text` | 3px solid | the two files' figures side by side, the differing ones bold |
| Not verified | triangle (scan glyph on rows) | NOT VERIFIED | `--evidence-unverified-fg` | 3px solid | count and reason |
| Confirmed / accepted / picked | signature / stamp | CONFIRMED BY YOU ... | `--doc-3-fg` / primary | none | the note, Undo |
| Matches | ticked circle | MATCHES | `--doc-3-fg` | none | folded |

Why Couldn't check is its own state: it shows no computed sum and no difference, because there is nothing honest to
subtract. The dashed rule and dashed circle echo the viewer's dashed passage box and the queue's Needs review mark
(the sheet with the dashed citation box): "look at the paper". The word says what happened to the check, not to the
review (the review-level word stays Needs review).

Row states (a second line under the claimant, with the source chip): Not verified (scan glyph, dashed chip), Conflicting
(scales, inset rule on the claim cell), Confirmed (signature), Edited (pencil; the extracted value shown struck beside
the new one), Added by you (no chip), Left out (eraser; amounts muted, "Left out of totals" with the note), Incurred
differs / Printed twice (warning diamond; counted as printed, tooltip gives both figures). Rows that do not count show
muted amounts. Superseded valuations fold under their claim ("Older valuation, Driftwood 2024, valued 12/31/2024, 3
lines" with its chip, struck amounts). Claim status is the plain uppercase word (OPEN, CLOSED, REOPENED; an unknown
spelling is printed as read, e.g. "Pending Review"); the printed spelling is the tooltip.

Not drawn in the comps (no fixture state): edited, added, left out rows. Their look is above; phase B builds them.

## 5. Directions for the review page

### A. Register and ledger (`a.html`)

The Certificates layout carried to numbers: the **claims register** fills the page and the **check ledger** sits in the
right column (380px), in need order: To resolve, Handled by you, Matches (folded). The **totals are the register's own
footing**: a "Counted totals" row pinned under the column headers, each figure under its column (paid under Paid,
incurred under Incurred), with "56 claims, 33 open, valued as of 09/30/2025" and an UNRECONCILED tag while a check is
open; recoveries and deductibles (hidden columns by default) sit in one sentence above. A check's "Show the 9 rows" scopes
the register to the rows that total covers and adds the reconciliation as a sticky footing under them (Rows above add
up to / Printed with its chip / OFF BY), so the broker sees the gap at the foot of the rows like on the carrier's page.
- **Totals strip**: column-aligned footing row, sticky.
- **Checks**: ledger cards with the figures table; Couldn't check is dashed and shows "Not checked".
- **Table at scale**: one sticky header, rows virtualized in the register's own scroll, 50 rows per API page (D2),
  sort and quick filters on the server (D10), quick filter counts beside each filter ("Over $100K 0": honest empty state
  naming the largest claim). 1,800 lines (lr_long) or 200k rows scroll in place; the footing never moves.
- **1024**: checks first in two columns, the register below at full width (Loss date column hidden).
- **Phone**: totals list, then the checks by word and title.
Screens: `qa-screenshots/loss-runs/directions/a-1440.png`, `a-1440-scope.png`, `a-1024.png`, `a-390.png`.
Weak points: two-line rows (chip under the claimant) cut density to about 10 rows per screen at 1440; the ledger
competes with the table for width at 1280 to 1440.

### B. Workpaper (`b.html`)

The review read the way an auditor ticks and ties a carrier's loss run. On top, **Files and totals**: one row per file
(chip, valued date, lines, its result: CONFLICTING / OFF BY / COULDN'T CHECK / NOT VERIFIED and "3 of 4 lines tie",
counted incurred), closed by the grand **Counted totals** row: totals strip and check index in one table. Below, one
**schedule per file** (serif carrier name), need-first, open when it has open items and folded when everything ties.
Inside, the rows in printed order and **every printed total footed exactly where the carrier printed it** (after the
2022 rows, at the foot of page 2), with its tick (MATCHES) or its two extra lines (rows add up to, difference) and the
actions in place. Superseded rows stay in their own file's schedule, struck, because that file's printed total still
counts them (spec section 4 step 6). A "By file / All claims" switch gives the flat sortable table.
- **Totals strip**: the summary table's last row.
- **Checks**: inline footings; there is no separate list (the summary is the index).
- **Table at scale**: weakest here. Per-file schedules are natural for 8 files and 120 pages, but 200k rows across 8
  files means each open schedule pages on its own and cross-file questions ("open claims over $100K") need the All
  claims view, which is then direction A's table without the ledger.
- **Phone**: totals list and the file summary as a list.
Screens: `b-1440.png`, `b-1440-footing.png`, `b-1024.png`, `b-390.png`.
Weak points: the work starts below the fold (the summary first); two ways to see claims; long files make long pages.

### C. Exceptions desk (`c.html`)

The review opens on its exceptions. A quiet **statement line** of six figures (each "from N claims", UNRECONCILED),
then tabs like the real product's Table view: **Checks (4 to resolve)**, **Claims (65)**, **Files (8)**. Each open check
is a **case** that sets the printed line, drawn as a strip of the carrier's paper with the cited figures in the viewer's
dashed box, beside "From the rows" (the rows in scope with their sum and the difference). The conflict shows the two
files' lines as two paper strips with "Use <file>" under each; the scan rows are listed with Confirm / Fix / Leave out
per row. The Claims tab is the full table, paged "Rows per page 50, 1 to 50 of 65" as in the evidence frames.
- **Totals strip**: a statement line over the tabs.
- **Checks**: the richest evidence per check; Couldn't check shows the printed strip and "Not checked".
- **Table at scale**: plain paging on its own tab; simple and close to the real product, but the checks and the table
  are never on screen together, so "Show the 9 rows" switches tabs.
- **Phone**: totals list and the cases by word and title.
Screens: `c-1440.png`, `c-1440-claims.png`, `c-1024.png`, `c-390.png`.
Weak points: the paper strip is a redrawing of the source, not the source (the chip opens the real page); the table is a
second-class tab although it is the thing the broker sends to the underwriter.

## 6. Recommendation: A, Register and ledger, with B's footing

First principles: the broker's output is the claims table (it goes to the underwriter), and their job on this page is
to make its totals trustworthy. So the table must be the page, and every open check must be one step from the rows it
is about. A is the only direction where the table, the totals and the checks are all on screen at 1440 and the check
"Show the rows" lands on the rows with the reconciliation at their foot, which is B's best idea (the footing where the
carrier printed it) carried in as a scoped state. It is also the most reuse: the Certificates ledger, its groups,
fold and note editor, the queue row status. The totals as the table's own footing keeps the strip from becoming a
row of stat tiles (the generic dashboard the owner rejected) and makes each total read as the sum of the column under it.

Take from C: the paper strip for the printed line, as an option inside a ledger card when the broker expands it (phase B
can test it). Take from B: per-file results ("3 of 4 lines tie") in the left panel's file rows, which already list the
files.

## 7. Owner calls (only the ones that change the build)

1. **Direction**: A (recommended), B or C.
2. **Couldn't check wording**: "COULDN'T CHECK" (recommended; matches DG3's "Couldn't verify") or "NEEDS REVIEW" (the API
   verdict, but the same words as the review's queue state).
3. **Checks grouped by printed line**: the API sends one check per printed total (Driftwood's line is three checks:
   paid, reserve, incurred). Recommend the UI groups them into one card per printed line with one Accept (sending
   three PATCHes). Alternative: one card per metric, as sent.
4. **Quick filter counts** ("Open only 38"): `GET .../claims` returns `total` for one query only, so counts need four
   extra calls per review open or a small `filter_counts` field from the backend. Recommend the backend field (counts
   from `build_ledger`); without it the filters show no counts.
5. **Action speed at 200k rows** (HANDOFF: about 9 s per broker action): the toast and the optimistic row change land
   at once; the totals footing shows "Recounting..." until the review returns. Recommend this over blocking the page;
   incremental rebuild stays a TODOS entry.
